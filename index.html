ADHD looks different for everyone

Scattered focus, trouble finishing what you start, feeling overwhelmed by tasks that seem simple to others. We'll spend time understanding your specific patterns first, then build from there - not the other way around.

What ADHD can actually look like

It rarely looks like the stereotype. Four patterns clients recognize themselves in.

The scattered starter

Ten projects going, none finished. Starting feels easy - following through is where it falls apart.

The last-minute sprinter

Nothing happens until the deadline is breathing down your neck, then somehow it gets done.

The quiet overthinker

Less "bouncing off the walls," more racing thoughts and trouble filtering out what doesn't matter.

The late realization

Spent years thinking you were just lazy or scattered, until a diagnosis reframed the whole story.

It's not a willpower problem.

ADHD is how your brain is wired, not a character flaw. The work isn't about trying harder - it's about building a life that actually fits how you think.

How we'll actually work together

Understanding your patterns comes first - how ADHD actually shows up for you, not a textbook version.

From there, we work on building systems that fit how your brain operates, instead of fighting it.

And along the way, we don't ignore what rides along with it - the anxiety and self-criticism that often come as a package deal.

Trauma and PTSD

Trauma-focused care, including EMDR, in a safe and steady space - moving at whatever pace actually feels okay for you.

The goal is for difficult memories to carry less weight in your day-to-day life.

Trauma can shape how safe the world feels long after the event itself is over. I'm trained in both EMDR and TF-CBT, two of the most well-supported approaches for helping the brain and body process difficult memories.

Past events or PTSD symptoms · EMDR & TF-CBT · In-person or online, your pace

What a session actually looks like

EMDR can sound mysterious from the outside. Here's the shape of it in practice.

We start with safety

Before anything else, we make sure you feel steady and in control of the pace.

We process at your pace

Using EMDR or TF-CBT, we work through what's ready to be worked through - never more than that.

We check in and integrate

We close with time to settle, and track how things are actually shifting over time.

There's no requirement to relive it

You don't have to describe what happened in detail for this work to be effective. EMDR in particular is built around that - your nervous system can heal without you narrating every moment out loud.

No. We move at whatever pace feels safe, and EMDR in particular doesn't require you to describe the event in detail for it to work.

Yes - EMDR can be effective for both recent and long-past events. The brain can still reprocess old memories even years later.
